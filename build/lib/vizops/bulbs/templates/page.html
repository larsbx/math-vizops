Bulbs & Ford Circles

The satellite bulbs of the Mandelbrot set, the Ford circles they rhyme with, and the finite certificates that pin them down

Scene 1

The satellite at internal angle p/q

Drag the red c. Green balls: the critical orbit 0, c, c²+c, …Blue α: the fixed point the cycle turns around.

The family is fc(z) = z² + c. On the main cardioid, c(λ) = λ/2 − λ²/4, where λ is the multiplier of the fixed point α. Where λ reaches the root of unity λ₀ = e2πip/q, a satellite bulb Bp/q buds off.

Inside Bp/q the critical orbit settles on a q-cycle, and that cycle turns around α by p/q of a revolution per step. Put c at the centre and count the steps between neighbouring balls.

certified The centre lies in a box of half-width 2−64. On that box a Krawczyk inclusion proves Qq has exactly one simple root there, and the collision exclusions prove its period is exactly q (P3).

validated The step-p rotation you can see here is checked in floating point at all 79 certified centres. It is what the label “this centre belongs to Bp/q” rests on.

Scene 2

Ford circles against bulb sizes

Top: the Ford circle C(p/q) touches the line at p/q with radius 1/(2q²). Click one.Bottom: red r is the root, green −1 the antipode (ρ = −1), dashed orange the Ford-scaled first-order size.

Two Ford circles touch exactly when |ps − qr| = 1, which is the Farey neighbour relation. Satellite bulbs attach to the cardioid in the same Farey order, and their size follows the Ford law to leading order:

d(p/q) = 2|c′(λ₀)| q⁻² · G(p/q)

For Ford circles that factor would be identically 1. For bulbs it is not: G is the correction this project studies. Here d is measured from the root to the antipode, the boundary point where the cycle multiplier is exactly −1.

certified The antipode comes from a two-variable Krawczyk box for (fcq(z) − z, (fcq)′(z) + 1). G is then bracketed through squared distances, G² = q⁴·|c − croot|² / |1 − λ₀|², so no square root is taken until the last step (P10).

import Reading the certified point as the antipode of Bp/q uses the Douady–Hubbard multiplier map [DH], which gives ∂Bp/q exactly one point with ρ = −1.

Scene 3

Wakes: doubling turns the circle by p/q

Angle doubling θ ↦ 2θ mod 1 has exactly one cycle that it moves like a rotation by p/q. It is built from the mechanical word

b_k = [ k·p mod q ≥ q − p ], k = 0 … q−1

read as a binary fraction over 2q − 1. Press Double: every ball jumps p places round the circle. The shortest gap, marked in red, is the characteristic arc (θ₋, θ₊). Its width is exactly 1/(2q − 1).

exact For every q ≤ 16 the tests check that doubling acts as this rotation, that the arc has that width, and that the wakes are disjoint and ordered like Farey fractions, with each mediant's wake in the gap between its neighbours (P4).

import The two parameter rays at θ₋ and θ₊, traced on the right, land at the root of Bp/q [DH, Mil00]. Uniqueness of the rotation cycle is [Gol92].

Scene 4

The parabolic flower and its index

The filled Julia set of g at the root. Green: the critical orbit from −λ₀/2 creeping into the petals. Blue 0: the parabolic point.

At the root, shift the coordinate so the parabolic point sits at 0: with z = λ₀/2 + w, the map becomes exactly

g(w) = λ₀·w + w², g^q(w) = w + a·w^(q+1) + …

Orbits approach 0 along q petals. The holomorphic index is a finite coefficient of that jet:

w − g^q(w) = w^(q+1)·P(w), ι = [w^q] 1/P(w)

It controls the bulb's second-order shape (P2):

ρ = 1 − q²ε + q³(ι − ½)ε² + O(ε³), λ = λ₀e^ε

exact The value ι lives in ℚ(ζq). For q ≤ 8 the exact coordinates above come from the finite-math-kernels cyclotomic reference, and are replayed against separate exact code and an Arb ball (P5). The inset plots every ιp′/q: they are Galois conjugates, ιp′/q = σp′(ι1/q).

Scene 5

How a box certifies a centre

Take a square β around a candidate centre m and the Gleason polynomial Qq (Q₀ = 0, Qn+1 = Qn² + C). The Krawczyk image is

K(β) = m − A·Q_q(m) + (1 − A·Q_q′(β))(β − m), A ≈ 1/Q_q′(m)

If K(β) lands strictly inside β (green on the left), β holds exactly one root, and it is simple. On the same β, every forbidden pair (i, j) must have 0 ∉ Qj(β) − Qi(β). Those are the green balls on the right, and together they rule out every period below q. The blue ball (0, q) is the one intended collision. Widen the box and watch both tests break.

certified This scene uses floating-point intervals so it can move. The stored certificates use exact rational intervals with outward dyadic rounding at r = 2−64, and every box is replayed from its endpoints alone. A failed check means inconclusive, never “no root”.

Scene 6

The modular inverse organises G

All 504 bulbs with q = 1009 and p ≤ q/2. Hover a ball.

Sorted by the angle p/q the correction G looks like noise. Sorted by the modular inverse, x̃ = p̄/q with p·p̄ ≡ 1 (mod q) and x* = |x̃|, the same numbers fall onto one curve. It runs from about 1.01 near 0 to about 1.16 near 2 − φ, with a downward cusp at every rational. The Ford analogy holds for the combinatorics and at leading order; at the first correction the modular group enters through p ↦ p⁻¹ (mod q).

validated These values are floating point (FFT on the Taylor data, cross-checked against Arb balls to 10−12). The curve is a function of x* up to about 10−3, and the bulbs with small p sit slightly above it (register V15–V21, F4).
