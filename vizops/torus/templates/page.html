Rauzy fractals on the 2-torus

For a unimodular Pisot substitution σ on {0, 1, 2}, project the stepped line of its fixed point along the expanding direction onto the plane H = {x₀ + x₁ + x₂ = 0}. The images are the discrepancy vectors Dn = pn − n·f, and modulo the lattice Hℤ they are the orbit of the toral rotation by −(f₀, f₁). Every number below is the research repository's exact kernel output.

diagnosticA lift profile certifies no covering degree: a finite sample on one grid sees two or three lifts on boundary cells even for Tribonacci, whose covering degree is 1 by theorem, and can miss lifts in cells it has not visited. Nothing here is a balanced-pair or coincidence certificate.

1The fractal in H

Each point is one prefix of the fixed point, at the floored coordinates the bundle carries; its colour is the next letter, so the three colours are the subtiles R(0), R(1), R(2).

x, y = ⌊2¹⁶ · hi_i / 2⁴⁰⌋, 2⁴⁰·D_ni ∈ [hi_i − n, hi_i]

Coordinates are (D₀, D₁) in the basis e₀ − e₂, e₁ − e₂ of Hℤ. This is a linear image of the contracting-plane picture, so lengths and angles in it mean nothing; the dashed lines are the lattice the next view folds by.

2Folded onto T² = H / Hℤ

Each cell of the G × G grid is coloured by the number of distinct ℤ²-lifts of it that the sample meets, as the bundle records it, cell for cell. A covering of degree 1 would show one lift almost everywhere; the cells with more sit on tile boundaries at this resolution.

lifts[cx·G + cy], cx = ⌊D₀ mod 1 · G⌋, cy = ⌊D₁ mod 1 · G⌋

The folded points are this page's display arithmetic, (x mod 2¹⁶)/2¹⁶; the bundle assigns a point to a cell only when its whole enclosure lies inside it, and counts the rest as undecided.

3The same fold, on a torus

The grid of view 2, wrapped onto a torus in space: D₀ mod 1 runs around the hole, D₁ mod 1 around the tube. The embedding and the shading are display aids; only the cell colours carry data.

(u, v) ↦ ((R + r cos 2πv) cos 2πu, (R + r cos 2πv) sin 2πu, r sin 2πv)

4The corpus, by rotation vector

The unimodular specimens of the standing corpus, at their rotation vectors ρσ on T² = [0, 1)², coloured by regime as the bundle names it. Specimens sharing a rotation vector share a marker, its area proportional to their number. Hover or tap one for its specimens and their lift profiles, copied as written.

ρ_σ = −(f₀, f₁) mod ℤ², placed at ((2⁴⁰ − F_i) mod 2⁴⁰) / 2⁴⁰
